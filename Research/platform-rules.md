# Platform and copyright realities for clipping other people's videos

Date: 2026-10-22. NOT legal advice. [S] sourced, [I] inference, [U] unverified.

## Core point
Two separate issues: (1) copyright (can you use the footage at all), (2) platform monetisation/reach policy (will the platform pay you or show it). Permission fixes only part of (1) and does not fix (2) on YouTube. [S/I]

## Copyright
- Without permission, reuse is infringement unless a defence like fair use applies. Fair use is a case-by-case US defence (commentary, criticism, news, parody, "transformative" uses) and is not a safe harbour for "short clip of someone's video". YouTube's 2015 fair-use legal support covered only a handful of clear cases. [S: https://tubefilter.com/2015/11/19/youtube-legal-support-fair-use/ , 2015 news, current status [U]]
- I did not fetch the US Copyright Office fair-use page this session; read https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/ for the four factors. [U]
- Rule of thumb [I]: no permission = high risk, takedowns/strikes likely; explicit written permission (or a licence/campaign terms) = the safe route.

## YouTube
Reused content policy (primary source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392) [S]:
- Reviews "commentary, clips, compilations, and reaction videos"; reused content must add "significant original commentary, substantive modifications"; viewers should see a "meaningful difference".
- Not OK: clips of moments edited together with little or no narrative; short videos compiled from other social sites.
- OK: critical review with clips, reaction with commentary, rewritten script/new voiceover, content mainly featuring the uploader.
- "This policy applies even if you have permission from the original creator." Monetisation may be removed from the whole channel if violations are found or ownership is unclear.
- July 15, 2025 "inauthentic content" update (renamed repetitious content) targets mass-produced, templated, near-identical uploads. [S: https://gulfnews.com/technology/youtube-updates-monetisation-policies-ai-and-repetitive-content-ban-begins-july-15-1.500192660 and the help page]
Implication [I]: an automated pipeline posting raw clips of others' videos to YouTube Shorts is exactly the risky pattern, even with permission, for YouTube Partner Programme revenue. Posting clips of your OWN content, or adding real commentary/edit, is fine.

## TikTok
Creator Rewards Program: originality is a payout metric; reposted/watermarked content and clips from other platforms reported as ineligible; videos must be over 60 s; personal accounts only. All from third-party guides, not TikTok's official page. [S weak: https://blog.nuelink.com/tiktok-creator-rewards-program/ ; official page [U]]

## Instagram / Facebook
Meta recommends only the original when it finds duplicates, labels reposts with a link to the original, and stops recommending accounts that mostly post unoriginal content (first reels in 2024, later photos/carousels); edited/remixed content can qualify if enough creative input (added context text, new graphics, remix). [S: https://creators.instagram.com/recommendations-and-originality and https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/414773/instagram-algorithm-update-deprioritizes-unorigina.html ; exact dates of expansion [U]]

## How clippers legitimately earn
1. Clip your own long videos/podcasts (no permission problem; your pipeline's safest target). [I]
2. Clipping programs where the owner supplies footage and pays per views: Whop Content Rewards pays per 1,000 views after brand approval, brand sets rate/minimum/cap; brands provide the source content and clippers post on their own accounts. [S: https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/growth-marketing/content-rewards] One analysis of 50 campaigns (Sept 2026) reported a median $1.00 per 1,000 views, range $0.05-$3.00 [S: https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/what-is-clipping-1-dollar-per-1000-views-50-campaigns , third-party]. Licensing terms per campaign: read each campaign's rules [U]. Note platforms may still demote reposted content (see above) [I].
3. Direct deals: a creator pays you per clip or monthly retainer for managing their shorts channel (permission = contract). [I]
4. Selling the tool/service to creators. [I]

## Practical checklist for the user [I]
- Keep a written permission record per source creator.
- Add something: captions, hook title, commentary, context, not just a cut.
- Do not bulk-post identical templated clips across many channels.
- Posting someone's clips on YouTube with monetisation on needs more than permission (see policy quote).
