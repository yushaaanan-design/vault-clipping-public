# Wave 1: structural analysis of the top 25 clips on Jynxzi's own channel (2026-10-22)

Source: every clip read frame by frame (one frame per second), plus transcript, cuts and top comments. Evidence rows in [[Wave 1 working table]]. The 25 span 8.7M to 44.2M views. SEEN = read by eye, FACT = counted from the table, INFERENCE = my reading. Sample is the top 25 only: it says what the BEST clips look like, not what separates them from flops (that needs the flop and outside waves).

## 1. The big finding: three families, not one format
| Family | Clips | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| A. Facecam over gameplay ("reaction") | 14 split-screen (#1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8,9,15,16,18,20,24) plus 2 cheap TikTok re-uploads (#19, #22) | face zoomed on top, game below. Inside this family there are two sub-types: A1 "watching a clip" (the phone clip-player UI is visible, play ring, heart, View comments; #2,5,6,7,9,15,18) and A2 his own live play (#1,3,4,8,20,24) |
| B. IRL / collab / talk | #10,11,12,13,14,17,21,23,25 (9 clips) | no gameplay at all: couch banter with CaseOh, a guest's story (Tyler1), fan-gift unboxing, a phone call with a viewer's grandma, a setup tour, a sports challenge |
| C. Cheap re-uploads of TikToks | #19, #22 | small strip with black bars, TikTok watermark, one pinned label; 9.7M and 9.4M anyway |
FACT: 16 of 25 contain gameplay, 9 of 25 have none. INFERENCE: the channel's reach is not only game clips; a clipper that only does facecam-plus-gameplay would miss more than a third of the top 25.

## 2. Structure (what happens in time)
- **One idea per clip.** FACT: 15 of 25 have 5 or fewer cuts; the 22M, 27M and 44M clips have 5, 2 and 2 cuts. The clips with many cuts (13-17) are the talk clips (CaseOh banter) where the cut follows the speaker.
- **Setup, escalation, payoff** (SEEN in most): a bragging or deadpan setup, escalating face, a held shock face or scream at roughly 60-80% of the length, then the payoff line or on-screen proof ("LOST ROUND 1", "WON ROUND 4", "2800", "Welcome to the rank squad").
- **Irony and reversal** are the strongest story shape: brags then loses (#3, 27M), expected failure then success (#7, #4), "doesn't realise it's his own clip" (#5, 15M).
- **Length does NOT decide it** (FACT): range 10 to 60 s, median about 34 s; 44M at 10 s, 22M at 58 s, 15M at 60 s. The earlier guess "short wins" (H1) is dropped.
- **Dead air is fine when tension carries it**: #4 is only 22% voiced and got 22M.

## 4. Design and positioning (the part to copy)
- **Zones** (SEEN): in family A the face occupies the top 32 to 42% and the seam sits at about y 0.35 to 0.42; gameplay fills the rest. The face is cropped very tight (eyes to chin fill the width). Families B and C are full-frame or a thin strip.
- **Caption systems, three of them** (FACT counts): (1) word-by-word subtitles, white italic comic-style font with heavy black outline, centred, 1-3 words per beat, placed at the seam or mid-height (about 13 clips); some add a YELLOW key-word highlight (#7, #21) or asterisk action text (*SMASHES XBOX*, *DAD STREAMSNIPER*, *CASTLES WINDOW*); (2) one pinned neon-blue comic title with dark outline for superlative claims (#4 "200 IQ CLUTCH", #9 "BEST STRAT OF THE YEAR", #15 "GIVES A CLIP A 10/10"); (3) one pinned white rounded label with the premise or a quoted line (#5, 18, 19, 20, 22, 24).
- **Speaker colours** (SEEN in 3 clips): in two-person or call clips each speaker gets a colour: white for him, yellow for CaseOh (#12, #14), yellow for him and purple for the grandma (#23), red/white for the storyteller and host (#10).
- **Inserts and proof** (SEEN): red circles on the wall (#22), a red "watch till end" tag (#22), logo flashes, on-screen game text as proof (WON ROUND 4, LOST ROUND 1, 1 VS 1), the original clip's player UI, a real photo or prop when a key noun is said.
- **Outros**: TV-static glitch or "SUBSCRIBED" button animation appear on some clips (#9, plus the flop in the earlier set); not needed for the top performers.
- **Polish is not the driver** (FACT): #19 and #22 are small, letterboxed, watermarked TikTok re-uploads and still reached 9.7M and 9.4M. This contradicts the earlier reading that black bars or a small window meant a weak clip (the 32k compilation); that weak one differed by weak content and in-joke labels.

## 5. What the comments quote (FACT from top comments)
Nearly every top comment quotes ONE specific line or moment ("So let me get this-" 111k, "Amaru didn't work" 59k, "Welcome to the rank squad" 95k, "I want 2 number 9's" 14k, "most respectful man for a solid minute" 107k). Others add missing context ("Sketch and CaseOh could use all guns, Jynxzi only his knife") or correct a detail ("that's a karambit, not an azami" 43k), which feeds engagement. A clip that produces a quotable line is a clip people comment on.

## 6. Topics and people (FACT)
CaseOh or Sketch appear in 3 of 25 (#4, #12, #14); Tyler1 in 1; Clix elsewhere. Fan gifts and unboxing: 2 (#13, #17). "Watching a better or funnier player and judging them": 7. Skill or exploit information (new spawn peek, grenade lineup, sledge wall, best strat): 5 (#7, #9, #19, #22 plus the lineup). Self-focused body or flirty humour: none in the top 25 (it sits at the bottom of the channel). An innuendo clip (#16) still reached 10.4M, so the line is the framing, not the topic alone.

## 7. Titles do not describe the clip (FACT)
#2 "Reacts to Player Thats Better Than Him" is actually the "you are not James Bond" rant; #18 "Where Does Darth Maul get his shoes" is a Comedy Night clip. INFERENCE: the title is a curiosity hook, the on-screen pinned label does the real explaining.

## 8. What this does NOT yet show
- No flops in this sample, so none of the above is proven to CAUSE views.
- Only his own channel; outside clips (other creators' edits) come next.
- The caption and zone numbers are read by eye, not measured; the automatic measures are unreliable.

## 10. Audio (measured from the signal, 2026-10-22; I do not listen)
Method: per-second loudness in dB from the clip's own audio, summarised per clip (typical level, loudest second, seconds that are 8 dB or more above the typical level = peaks, first 3 s versus the typical level, share of near-silent seconds, dynamic range, mean spectral centroid = brightness). It cannot tell music from speech or name a sound effect; speech share comes from the transcript. Series per clip: `study/data/<id>/audio.json`. n = 25 top clips, so everything is FACT for these 25 and only an INFERENCE beyond them. Rank correlations (Spearman) are within the top 25 only, where the views range is narrow, so they are weak evidence.
- **No single audio hook** (FACT): the first 3 seconds are louder than the clip's typical level (more than +2 dB) in 8 of 25, quieter in 7, about the same in 10; median difference +1.5 dB; correlation with views 0.06 (nothing). The hook is carried by the picture and the words, not by a volume jump.
- **Peaks are not required** (FACT): 10 of 25 clips have no scream or effect peak at all (the 44.2M clip is one: it is loud and compressed the whole way, typical level -8 dB). 10 clips have 5 or more peak seconds (screams and effects: #2, #3, #4, #5, #8, #11, #13, #15, #16, #17). Peak count vs views 0.21 (weak).
- **Where the loudest second falls** (FACT): last third of the clip in 10, first third in 10, middle in 5. Two shapes: front-loaded (a loud opening line) or end-loaded (the payoff scream or reveal in the last third, e.g. #2, #5, #8, #16, #23).
- **Dead air hurts, a little** (FACT, weak): share of near-silent seconds vs views -0.52, but only 5 of 25 clips have more than 5% silence (#5, #9, #13, #17, #18); the worst, #13, still reached 11.8M. INFERENCE: continuous sound or speech keeps the top clips together; a clip that goes quiet loses viewers, but this sample is too narrow to be sure.
- **Dynamic range**: median 20 dB; wider contrast between quiet and loud goes with slightly higher views (0.30). Very flat clips (#22: 4.7 dB, #24: 3.5 dB) are the re-uploads or one-take clips. INFERENCE: contrast (calm then scream) is a pattern in the strong clips, but flat clips also hit 9M.
- **Loudness level**: typical level ranges -39 to -8 dB, median -25; louder overall does NOT mean more views (-0.21). The three loudest clips are the heavily compressed ones: the 44.2M "green light" clip (-8 dB), and the two TikTok re-uploads (#19 -12 dB, #22 -11 dB).
- **Brightness** (centroid): about 1.8 kHz median, 1.35 to 2.2 kHz range, no relation to views (0.17).
- **Reading**: the audio of a top clip is continuous voice with at least one clear contrast moment, not a special sound. For a clipper: pick windows with continuous speech, avoid dead air, and look for the loudest moment as the payoff, then end shortly after it.
